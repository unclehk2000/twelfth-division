[b]Hadō 99[/b] puts two things on one chart, each on its own card:
• [b]Macro:[/b] which macro force the index is moving with today, the dollar or oil, in one of three tested regimes; how hard it is pulling, in index points per 1% move; and the range to expect today.
• [b]Fractal:[/b] the C2 → C3 Fractal Model drawn on price: the sweep, C2's EQ and T-spot, the CISD and its five projections, the protected swing, with odds for every step.
• [b]HTF candles (PO3):[/b] the higher-timeframe candles of the pair right of price, tagged C1–C4, in the model's colours, ICT's green and black, or your own bodies, borders and wicks.
Every number on both cards was measured on NQ, ES and RTY 2018–26. A result is only shown if it held at p < 0.05 (errors clustered by date), in both 2018–24 and 2025–26, and on each of the three markets. [i]It is a map of how these setups and regimes usually behave, not a signal generator.[/i]


[b]█ MACRO CARD[/b]

[b]The regime[/b] (the header pill), worked out from yesterday's closes:
• [b]WEAK $[/b]: DXY below its 200-day average. The index follows oil: 1% on CL ≈ +0.16 to +0.22% on the index. The dollar matters little.
• [b]STRONG $[/b]: DXY above its 200-day average. The dollar leads: 1% on DXY ≈ −0.75 to −0.95%. Oil barely matters.
• [b]OIL SHOCK[/b]: CL's front month 15%+ a year above the second month, and CL up 10%+ in 20 days. Both turn against the index: 1% DXY ≈ −1.05 to −1.50%, 1% CL ≈ −0.10 to −0.13%. Together they explain about 30% of the day's move, against 6–13% in the other regimes.
• Share of days 2018–26: weak 42%, strong 52%, shock 6% (28% so far in 2026).

[b]Rows[/b]
• [b]WATCH:[/b] what to watch in this regime, and the index points per 1% move in it.
• [b]RANGE:[/b] the range to expect today in points, and how much of it is used so far. For sizing targets and stops (see below).
• [b]DXY[/b] and [b]OIL:[/b] each one's move against its prior close, and the index move that goes with it in this regime, in % and points.
• [b]TODAY:[/b] the index's move, and how much of it the dollar and oil account for.
• [b]LIVE β:[/b] the same two-factor betas measured on the last 60 days, next to the tested ones. Far apart means the live link has drifted.
• [b]SHOCK:[/b] how far CL is from the oil-shock rule.
• [b]CARRY:[/b] the index carry (next contract over front, annualised), and the points a day the front month gives up to the cash index.
• Mobile layout keeps WATCH, RANGE, DXY and OIL. The rest stays in the tooltips.
• An alert fires when the regime changes.

[b]RANGE: what it is built on[/b]
• The regime itself did [i]not[/i] change the day's range. A big dollar or oil day did.
• After a day when DXY or CL moved 2+ daily standard deviations (about 1 day in 10), the next day ran [b]0.10–0.19× ADR20 larger[/b] than the index's own recent range implied (p < 0.01, both periods, every symbol).
• Expected range = ADR20 × the average range / ADR20 that followed a day like yesterday. Rows are yesterday's own range (under 1, 1–1.5, and 1.5+ × ADR20):
  – normal day: 0.92 / 1.06 / 1.33×;
  – after a 2+ sd DXY or CL day: [b]1.07 / 1.16 / 1.51×[/b].
• DXY's overnight move added nothing to the New York session's range once the index's own overnight range was known, so it is not used.

[b]What the macro card does not do[/b]
• Neither DXY nor CL led the index: not over the next minutes, the rest of the day, the next session or the next week.
• A gap between the index's move and what the dollar implies did not close by 16:00.
• The carry did not lead the next week's return, its range, where it closed, or a take of the prior week's high or low.
[i]So the regime, WATCH, DXY, OIL and TODAY are context: what is moving the index and by how much. RANGE is the forward-looking row.[/i]


[b]█ FRACTAL MODEL: WHAT IT DRAWS ON PRICE[/b]

Bullish shown; bearish is the mirror image.
• [b]C2[/b]: a higher-timeframe candle sweeps the previous candle's (C1's) low and closes back above it. It is labelled at its low, with a line from C1's low to the sweep. If C2's low is later broken, the label turns to [b]XC2[/b] and the setup keeps only its label, the sweep and a grey CISD line.
• [b]EQ and T-spot[/b]: C2's midpoint, and the zone from C3's open to EQ, shaded during C3.
• [b]CISD[/b]: the highest open of the run of down-closing bars that delivered C2's low (up to 10 bars back). It is confirmed by the first close above it, and labelled [i]CISD · in C2[/i] when that happens inside C2. While C2 is still forming, an [b]early CISD[/b] shows as a dashed line.
• [b]Projections[/b]: CISD + k × (CISD − C2's low) for k = +1, +2, +2.5, +4 and +4.5, each with its odds, ticked ✓ when hit.
• [b]Protected swing[/b] (●): after the CISD, the first down-closing bar arms a level at its open. The level moves to the open of the down run into each new low. A close back above it marks the lowest 3-bar swing low in between. Greyed if taken before C4 ends.
[b]Pairs[/b]
• [b]1m chart, 15-minute candles[/b].
• [b]1H chart, daily candles[/b] (the CME day, 18:00–17:00 ET).
• [i]Auto[/i] picks 15-minute candles on charts of 5 minutes or less, and daily candles on longer intraday charts.
• Other chart timeframes still run, but the card marks the odds [i]untested[/i].


[b]█ HTF CANDLES (PO3)[/b]

The pair's last higher-timeframe candles (5 by default, 2–10) right of price, oldest on the left; the one still forming sits on the right with a dashed border.
• [b]Tags:[/b] C1–C4 of the latest setup, under the candles for a bullish setup and over them for a bearish one.
• [b]Times:[/b] each candle's open (15-minute) or day (daily) under it; the pair and a countdown to the close over the candles.
• [b]Colours:[/b]
  – [i]Model[/i]: the setup's blue and red, see-through bodies;
  – [i]ICT[/i]: green up bodies, black down bodies, black borders and wicks (white instead of black on a dark chart);
  – [i]Custom[/i]: your own body, border and wick colour, for up and down candles.
• [b]Shape:[/b] wick width; candle width and the gap between candles (auto: 6 and 3 bars on 1–2 minute charts, 4 and 2 up to 5 minutes, 2 and 2 above); the offset from price. Wicks stop at the body, so a see-through body stays clean.
• [b]Optional lines:[/b]
  – the forming candle's open;
  – the last closed candle's high and low, from where they were made: the levels the forming candle must sweep and close back inside to become a C2;
  – separators on the chart where each candle opened.
  An open within a hair of that high or low shares its label.


[b]█ FRACTAL CARD[/b]

The header takes the colour of a live setup (blue bull, red bear), and goes grey when there is none. The pill shows the pair.
• [b]C2:[/b] side, time (or day), where C2 closed in its own range, and whether price is in C3, in C4 or done.
• [b]C3:[/b] the odds C3 takes C2's high, for that close location. Once C3 trades back to EQ, the lower EQ odds. A tick and the time when it happens.
• [b]C2 LOW[/b] / [b]C2 HIGH:[/b] the odds C2's swept extreme holds through C3, or the time it broke.
• [b]CISD:[/b] confirmed in C2 (early), or the time it confirmed, or waiting. With its price.
• [b]DXY[/b] (15-minute pair): DXY across the C2 candle, and whether it moved the setup's way.
• [b]TARGETS:[/b] the projections with their odds, ticked as they are hit.
• [b]PROTECT:[/b] the protected swing's price and its odds of holding to the end of C4, by bars left.
• Mobile layout keeps C2, C3, CISD and PROTECT.


[b]█ ALERTS[/b]

Create one alert with "Any alert() function call". It covers:
• the macro regime changing;
• a new C2 (with where it closed and the odds C3 takes its other extreme);
• a CISD confirmed;
• a protected swing marked.


[b]█ WHAT THE TESTS SHOWED (FRACTAL)[/b]

NQ, ES and RTY 1-minute data 2018–26: 248k C2s on 15-minute candles, 3,089 on daily candles. Figures are [b]15m / 1D[/b].

[b]Where C2 closes decides C3.[/b] C3 took C2's high, by C2's close location (bottom → top quarter of its range):
• 15m: 21 / 36 / 55 / [b]78%[/b];
• 1D: 20 / 39 / 58 / [b]83%[/b].
[i]Against other candles that closed in the same place, a C2 was not followed by more expansion (−1.6 / −3.2 points). The pattern adds nothing to C3 beyond where it closes.[/i]

[b]After C3 trades back to EQ[/b], the odds C3 still takes C2's high, by the same quarters:
• 15m: 21 / 35 / 47 / 64%;
• 1D: 19 / 38 / 52 / 70%.

[b]C2's swept low held through C3:[/b]
• 15m: 28 / 48 / 67 / 82%;
• 1D: 19 / 46 / 65 / 81%.
• That is [b]+1.7 / +3.4 points[/b] more often than other candles' lows at the same close location, and [b]+2.8 / +7.4[/b] when the CISD confirmed inside C2.

[b]CISD:[/b] confirmed inside C2 on 66 / 74% of C2s, and by the end of C4 on 88 / 91%. Once close location is accounted for, an early CISD does not raise the odds C3 takes C2's high.

[b]Projections[/b] reached before C4 ended, after the CISD confirmed:
• +1: [b]72 / 80%[/b];
• +2: 46 / 57%;
• +2.5: 36 / 49%;
• +4: 20 / 32%;
• +4.5: 16 / 28%.

[b]Protected swings[/b] (15-minute pair, 140k swings):
• They held to the end of C4 [b]9.5 points more often[/b] than other 1-minute swing lows with the same bars left and the same distance from price. 49% held overall.
• By bars left in C4 when marked: ≤5: [b]93%[/b]; 6–10: 77%; 11–15: 64%; 16–20: 52%; 21+: 39%.
• A CISD inside C2 added 4.8 points.
• [i]Not tested on daily candles; the card says so.[/i]

[b]DXY during C2[/b] (15-minute pair, 2023–26, 93k C2s):
• When DXY moved the setup's way by more than 0.5 sd (1 C2 in 4), C2's extreme held through C3 [b]1.7 points[/b] more often, and C3 took C2's other extreme 1.3 points more often, at the same close location.
• DXY moving against the setup changed nothing.
• [i]A small edge on top of the close-location odds, not a signal by itself.[/i] On daily candles, neither DXY nor CL made a difference.

[b]The macro regime made no difference to any fractal odds.[/b]

[b]█ HOW TO USE[/b]

• Built for CME index futures: [b]NQ / MNQ[/b], [b]ES / MES[/b] and [b]RTY / M2K[/b]. Micros use their full-size contract's tested values.
• Use a continuous, back-adjusted chart ([i]B-ADJ[/i]) with extended hours, so the daily candles and the ranges are the full CME day.
• [b]Fractal:[/b] a 1-minute chart for the 15-minute pair, a 1-hour chart for the daily pair. Those are the tested combinations.
• [b]Macro:[/b] works on any intraday chart. CL and DXY need their data on your plan; delayed data delays those rows.
• Give the two cards different places. If you also run Shikai / Bankai Session Map, set the HTF candles' offset to 70 or more so they sit beyond its session candles.
• For alerts, create one alert on [i]Any alert() function call[/i].


[b]█ DATA AND METHOD[/b]

[b]Macro[/b]
• NQ, ES and RTY daily data 2018–26.
• DXY: ICE daily closes; DX futures 5-minute bars (2023–26).
• CL: front and second-month settles; 1-minute bars around FOMC weeks.
• Regimes are set from yesterday's closes. The betas come from two-factor regressions of the index's daily % change on DXY's and CL's, by regime.
• RANGE: 6,500 symbol-days. Range = the CME day's high − low; ADR20 = the mean of the previous 20.

[b]Fractal[/b]
• NQ, ES and RTY front-month 1-minute data, Jan 2018 – Oct 2026.
• 15-minute candles from the 1-minute bars; daily candles (CME day) on 1-hour bars.
• Odds are plain frequencies. Each comparison is a linear probability model with errors clustered by date:
  – C2s against all other candles that closed in the same place;
  – protected swings against other 1-minute swings with the same bars left and distance.

[b]On the chart[/b]
• The higher-timeframe candles are built from the chart's own bars. That costs no extra data request and matches the tests.
• Daily macro values use finished bars only.
• DXY across C2 uses the finished 15-minute candle.
• Protected swings are marked on closed bars.
• Nothing sees future data.


[b]█ LIMITATIONS[/b]

[i]These are historical frequencies, not guarantees.[/i]
• The odds were measured on NQ, ES and RTY. Other symbols show the same tables without a test of their own.
• The DXY tests used DX futures; the card uses TVC:DXY by default (you can change it).
• Chart timeframes other than 1m (15-minute pair) and 1H (daily pair) are untested, and the card says so.
• Nothing here is financial advice.


[b]█ WHY THE NAME[/b]

[b]Hadō #99, Goryūtenmetsu[/b] (五龍転滅), sits at the top of the numbered destructive kidō. Only the strongest kidō users can cast it: it raises five great dragons from the caster's own reiatsu and brings them down on the target. Aizen turns it on Yhwach in the final arc.
This indicator climbed the kidō to get here. It began as [b]Tenteikūra[/b] (天挺空羅, Bakudō #77), the spell that carries one message to everyone at once: a single card relaying what the dollar and oil were saying. It became [b]Bakudō #58, Kakushitsuijaku[/b] (掴趾追雀), a tracking spell, when the card learned to follow the macro force the index was moving with. Bakudō binds and tracks; hadō strikes. With the Fractal Model built in, it outgrew the binding spells.
The five dragons are the CISD's five projections, +1, +2, +2.5, +4 and +4.5, raised from the setup's own leg. And as Yhwach saw Goryūtenmetsu coming and rewrote the outcome, no projection here is certain: each one carries its tested odds, from 72% for the first to 16% for the fifth on the 15-minute pair.
